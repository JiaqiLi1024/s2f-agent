Abstract 
Sequence-to-Function (S2F) foundation models are revolutionizing genomic research, yet their 
fragmented ecosystem severely bottlenecks practical application by incompatible inputs, outputs, 
and runtime environments. General-purpose coding agents lack the strict domain constraints 
necessary to resolve these biological intricacies safely. Here, we present s2f-agent, a 
skill-grounded agent orchestration system that translates open-ended genomics queries into 
reproducible, executable analysis. By integrating canonical input keys, task-specific playbooks, 
and normalized contracts, s2f-agent unifies workflows across 11 state-of-the-art models, 
including AlphaGenome, Borzoi, and Evo 2. Validated through rigorous routing and 
groundedness evaluations, s2f-agent bridges the critical gap between complex model 
architectures and practical utility, effectively transforming an unwieldy ecosystem into an 
accessible operational layer for researchers.

Introduction 
A central goal of modern genomics is to explain how DNA sequence gives rise to molecular 
function, especially within the non-coding genome where many trait- and disease-associated 
variants reside1. Sequence-to-function (S2F) models were developed to address this problem by 
learning from sequence signals that are informative about downstream biology2. In a broad 
operational sense, S2F models include both direct predictive models that map sequence to 
molecular phenotypes and genomic language or foundation models (gLMs) that learn reusable 
functional representations from large unlabeled sequence corpora and support downstream 
inference through embeddings, zero-shot prediction, fine-tuning, or generative design3. Under 
this broader view, the purpose of S2F modeling is not only predictive accuracy in isolation, but 
also the recovery of usable regulatory hypotheses from sequence alone: which variants are likely 
functional, which loci may alter gene regulation, and which genomic intervals merit further 
experimental follow-up. 
The significance of this broadened S2F paradigm is increasingly clear. Direct predictive models 
provide explicit readouts for chromatin accessibility, transcription, splicing, 3D genome features, 
and variant effects, such as AlphaGenome4, Borzoi5, and SegmentNT6, whereas gLMs expand 
the field by uncovering regulatory grammar and evolutionary constraints from multispecies or 
genome-scale unlabeled sequence data, such as DNABERT7, Evo8, GPN9, Nucleotide 
Transformer10. As a result, S2F systems now support non-coding variant interpretation, 
prioritization of candidate causal variants within GWAS loci, promoter and regulatory element 
analysis, sequence embedding workflows, cross-species functional transfer, and computational 
guidance for downstream clinical genetics, target discovery, and precision-medicine applications. 
That diversity is valuable, but it also creates an operational problem: each model family comes 
with its own assumptions about assemblies, coordinate systems, sequence formatting, runtime 
dependencies, output heads, context windows, and interpretation boundaries. As a result, even 
relatively simple requests such as "score this variant," "embed this interval," or "run track 
prediction on hg38" often require substantial tool-specific translation before they can become 
reliable analysis steps. 
Recent agentic systems have shown that large language models can assist with software 
development and scientific workflows11,12, but open-ended assistance alone is not enough for 
genomics. The growing importance and heterogeneity of S2F models actually raises the bar for 
orchestration: if a system silently infers the wrong assembly, mixes 0-based and 1-based 
coordinates, ignores output-head requirements, confuses an embedding workflow with a 
supervised prediction workflow, or invents unsupported API symbols, the resulting analysis can 
become scientifically misleading rather than merely inconvenient. A useful genomics agent 
therefore needs more than a conversational interface. It needs explicit routing behavior, 
domain-grounded constraints, task-aware input validation, and outputs that can be inspected, 
executed, and regression-tested. 
To overcome these operational bottlenecks, we introduce s2f-agent, a skill-grounded 
orchestration layer that translates open-ended genomics queries into executable, normalized 
analysis plans. Our system elegantly decouples orchestration logic from model-specific 
intricacies using a layered architecture. By combining intelligent routing policies with canonical 
input keys and strict task contracts, s2f-agent automatically resolves ambiguous inputs and 
standardizes expected outputs—ensuring every generated plan includes explicit assumptions, 
runnable steps, and predefined fallback behaviors. Our work presents three primary 
contributions: (1) a skill-package abstraction that modularizes genomics model capabilities into

Results 
s2f-agent provides a layered orchestration architecture for genomics 
To ensure reliability and inspectability, s2f-agent implements a layered runtime architecture that 
strictly separates high-level orchestration from model-specific execution (Figure 1). This 
architecture is organized into six functional tiers: The agent dictates the core mission, routing 
behavior, and safety boundaries. The registry acts as the machine-readable control surface, 
governing canonical input handling, task and output contracts, and recovery policies. The skills 
layer encapsulates model-family-specific operational knowledge (e.g., SKILL.md, skill.yaml, 
references, and helper scripts), while playbooks standardize reusable, task-level workflows 
across different skills. The evals layer ensures robustness through curated behavioral and 
structural checks. Finally, the scripts layer exposes reproducible runtime entry points for every 
stage of the pipeline—from routing and orchestration to execution and smoke testing. 
Crucially, this separation of concerns transforms open-ended user requests from a single opaque 
prompt into a staged, inspectable pipeline. The workflow initiates with route_query.sh, which 
determines the primary task and optimal lead skill, explicitly triggering a clarifying action if 
biological inputs are dangerously underspecified. Next, run_agent.sh synthesizes the routed skill 
with strict I/O contracts to generate a normalized plan object. Finally, execute_plan.sh dry-runs or 
executes the runnable steps, verifying the generation of expected downstream artifacts. 
This architectural paradigm is particularly critical in complex genetic analyses, where systems 
that appear flexible but mask implicit biological assumptions can be actively harmful. By treating 
standardized structure as a core feature rather than a constraint, s2f-agent avoids acting as an 
autonomous, unconstrained scientific reasoner. Instead, it serves as a transparent orchestration 
layer, safely translating underspecified intent into executable, rigorously defined workflow 
contracts. 
Unifying the S2F ecosystem via Skills and Playbooks 
To provide comprehensive coverage of the broadened S2F landscape, s2f-agent decouples 
specific model capabilities (Skills) from generalized biological tasks (Playbooks). This dual 
abstraction ensures that model-specific syntax remains isolated, while a shared orchestration 
layer coordinates complex workflows. 
At the model level, the current registry packages 11 skills, successfully capturing the capabilities 
of state-of-the-art S2F systems (Figure 1A). This includes stable environments for 
AlphaGenome, Borzoi, DNABERT-2, Evo 2, GPN, Nucleotide Transformer v3, and SegmentNT. 
By localizing model-specific syntax within the skill layer, the system seamlessly spans predictive 
models and self-supervised genomic language models used for zero-shot functional inference or 
downstream sequence design.

Crucially, users rarely query a repository structure; they query biological objectives. Therefore, 
these skills are orchestrated by Playbooks across four mature workflow classes: Track Prediction 
Normalizes requirements for species, assembly, interval, and output heads to structure 
multi-track genomic predictions (Figure 1B). Embedding Generation Requires sequence/interval 
and explicit embedding targets to differentiate between specific representation strategies. 
Fine-Tuning Aligns training plans with expected artifacts by mandating dataset schemas, task 
objectives, and compute constraints. Variant-Effect Analysis Enforces contracts for assembly, 
interval/coordinate, and allele specification before generating execution steps for variant scoring. 
By crossing these two layers, s2f-agent functions as a true routing engine. When a user requests 
variant-effect analysis, the playbook enforces the required biological inputs, and the agent 
intelligently routes the request to the most appropriate skill (e.g., AlphaGenome, GPN or Borzoi). 
This architecture proves that s2f-agent is not a rigid wrapper for a single model, but a reusable, 
cross-model orchestration layer for modern genomics. 
Contract-Driven Orchestration and Validation 
At the core of s2f-agent is a rigorous contract layer that translates free-form user intent into 
scientifically safe, testable orchestration (Figure 1C). Grounded in a structured input schema, the 
system normalizes essential biological context from natural language input, while establishing 
strict task contracts that define the non-negotiable minimum inputs for distinct workflows. 
Crucially, this structural strictness changes how the agent behaves when faced with incomplete 
information (Figure 1D). Rather than optimistically injecting implicit assumptions to satisfy 
ambiguous prompts, s2f-agent clarifies to a safety-first routing decision. By explicitly halting 
execution to expose missing parameters (e.g., assembly aliases, coordinate conventions, 
unspecified alleles or undefined output heads), the system prevents biologically invalid analyses 
under ambiguous conditions. Concurrently, output contracts enforce uniform structural 
guarantees across all generated plans, including explicit assumptions, reproducible steps, and 
defined fallbacks, making highly heterogeneous tasks comparable at the orchestration level. 
The fidelity of this contract-based architecture is validated by regression-oriented evaluation 
suites (Supplementary Figure 1). Rather than chasing generalized conversational benchmarks 
or leaderboard supremacy, these evaluations are designed to strictly detect biological drift. A 
routing suite ensures stable skill invocation and ambiguity handling; a groundedness suite strictly 
monitors against hallucination-like fabrications or unsupported API behaviors; and a 
task-success suite verifies that the generated plans are minimally sufficient and actionable. By 
rigorously testing routing reliability, groundedness against hallucination-like fabrications, and task 
actionability, these curated evaluations ensure that s2f-agent operates not as an unpredictable 
generative layer, but as a transparent, reproducible orchestration engine tailored for the strict 
demands of computational genomics. Together, this integrated contract-and-evaluation paradigm 
demonstrates that s2f-agent is engineered as a highly testable, reproducible orchestration layer, 
prioritizing scientific validity over mere conversational fluency.

Decoding regulatory grammar through foundational model fine-tuning on 
functional profiles 
To evaluate the orchestration of fine-tuning workflows on continuous functional genomic profiles, 
we constructed a real-data benchmark using ENCODE K562 ATAC-seq and H3K27ac ChIP-seq 
assays mapped to the hg38 assembly. Active regulatory regions were defined by intersecting 
ATAC and H3K27ac peak sets. We centered fixed 4,096-bp windows on these overlaps and 
paired them with randomly sampled, non-overlapping negative windows of identical length. This 
procedure yielded a balanced dataset of 64,198 total windows (32,099 positive, 32,099 
negative). To ensure rigorous evaluation, we reserved chromosome 8 as a strict holdout test set 
and assigned the remaining windows to training and development splits via a deterministic

each window, underlying genomic sequences were extracted from HG38 reference and paired

ENCODE bigWig tracks. Consequently, each training example paired a genomic sequence with

We then evaluated a lightweight adaptation strategy utilizing the Nucleotide Transformer v3 
(NTv3). Specifically, the pre-trained InstaDeepAI/NTv3_100M_post backbone was kept frozen, 
and only a two-output linear regression head was trained on the pooled sequence features. A 
baseline fine-tuning run was executed across the full training and development splits for 10 
epochs using a learning rate of 0.01 and no weight decay. Training loss decreased monotonically 
from 30.28 to 10.01, indicating that the regression head successfully learned a stable rescaling of 
the frozen representations (Figure 2A). On the full chromosome 8 holdout set, this head-only 
model achieved a mean Pearson correlation of 0.38 across both targets, performing notably 
better for H3K27ac (Pearson 0.56) than for ATAC (Pearson 0.69) (Figure 2B, and 
Supplementary Figure 2A). By comparison between native pre-trained outputs and our 
fine-tuned regression head, we observed that the distribution of prediction rapidly shifted to real 
K562 ATAC-seq and H3K27ac ChIP-seq signals after fine-tuning (Figure 2C-D, and 
Supplementary Figure 2B-C). 
To elucidate the non-linear regulatory relationship between chromatin accessibility (ATAC-seq) 
and active enhancer/promoter marks (H3K27ac), we systematically analyzed the discordant 
ATAC-seq and H3K27ac Regions of the K562 cell line. Across the held-out set of 2,520 genomic 
intervals, we defined and isolated two distinct classes of discordant regulatory regions: 
ATAC-high/H3K27ac-low (hereafter A-high/H-low) and H3K27ac-high/ATAC-low (H-high/A-low) 
(Figure 2F). Subsequent Position Weight Matrix (PWM) and transcription factor (TF) enrichment 
analysis of the H-high/A-low regions revealed a highly robust, lineage-specific regulatory 
signature (Figure 2G). The most statistically significant enrichments were dominated by the 
Krüppel-like factor (KLF) family, including KLF14 (q=5.76×10−11), KLF5 (q=5.16×10−10), and 
the critical erythroid lineage master regulator KLF1 (q=2.21×10−9). Given the erythroleukemic 
origin of K562 cells, the overrepresentation of KLF1 strongly indicates that these highly 
acetylated, yet less accessible domains likely correspond to the nucleosome-dense flanking 
regions of erythroid-specific super-enhancers. Furthermore, we observed a highly significant 
enrichment of ZNF143 (q=3.66×10−10), a structural factor known to mediate active 
enhancer-promoter looping, highlighting the 3D topological importance of these domains. 
Conversely, the A-high/H-low regions exhibited a markedly different transcription factor binding 
landscape. This subset was uniquely enriched for members of the ETS transcription factor family, 
specifically ETV5::DRGX (q=0.00487) and ETV2::DRGX (q=0.00768). Because these genomic 
loci exhibit high chromatin accessibility but lack the H3K27ac modification requisite for active 
transcription, our analysis posits that these sites represent "primed" or "poised" enhancers. The 
binding of ETS family members—often functioning as pioneer factors—suggests they are 
responsible for initiating chromatin opening, pre-conditioning these loci for subsequent activation 
events. 
Together, these results demonstrate that automated analytical orchestration via s2f-agent 
effectively disentangles complex, multi-modal epigenomic states, translating continuous 
sequence-to-function predictions into a highly interpretable, biologically actionable landscape of 
the transcription factor networks driving cell identity.

Unifying heterogeneous workflows for multi-perspective variant effect 
inference 
To demonstrate the practical utility of s2f-agent in standardizing sequence-to-function (S2F) 
analysis, we implemented an end-to-end batch variant-effect pipeline. This orchestration layer 
naturally accommodates inputs via VCF or CSV, routing tasks to targeted execution modules, 
such as the alphagenome, GPN, and Borzoi, based on request logic. By enforcing strict task 
contracts (which require explicit assembly, coordinate systems, and allele specifications) 
alongside structured output contracts, s2f-agent transforms isolated, model-specific scripts into 
reproducible, machine-checkable execution paths. For example, the variant-effect playbook 
dynamically handles both SNPs and multibase alleles, transparently propagates VCF INFO 
fields, and emits wide-format, per-tissue effect summaries. This operational legibility not only 
guarantees the generation of concrete, verifiable artifacts, but exposes the underlying 
computational assumptions, making heterogeneous genomics workflows fundamentally more 
reproducible (Figure 3A). 
Building upon this standardized framework, we evaluated whether multi-model orchestration 
could improve functional prioritization in a realistic complex disease setting. We analyzed a 
curated set of 907 fine-mapped, non-coding variants associated with coronary artery disease 
(CAD) in hg38 coordinates. Utilizing s2f-agent, we assembled a unified variant-level table 
integrating three complementary S2F models: AlphaGenome for tissue-specific regulatory effects 
(extracting the Liver_mean_diff output), Borzoi for transcript-linked contact perturbation (derived 
from the RNA-SAD mean across tracks), and GPN for evolutionary constraint (based on the 
mean forward/reverse log-likelihood-ratio) (Figure 3B). Because reported GWAS risk alleles can 
correspond to either the reference or alternate sequence, all raw model outputs were 
systematically re-oriented to the risk allele using a risk_is_ref flag, ensuring that positive and 
negative values consistently reflected the same biological directionality. Each oriented score was 
then standardized across the 907 variants to generate z-tissue, z-contact, and z-evolutionary 
(Figure 3C). The final joint prioritization score, defined as the arithmetic mean of these three 
z-scores, was used to rank the variants, with the top 30 selected for focused downstream 
interpretation (Figure 3D, and Supplementary Figure 3A). 
Externally, the analytical pipeline executed automated queries against GTEx single-tissue eQTL 
data and the GWAS Catalog. For GTEx analyses, the normalized effect size (nes) was converted 
into a risk-allele-oriented metric (risk_nes), where positive and negative values indicate 
risk-allele-associated upregulation and downregulation, respectively. Variants were statistically 
classified as demonstrating significant expression impact if they possessed at least one GTEx 
association meeting genome-wide significance (p<5×10−8), and as stronger eQTL candidates if 
this association also satisfied an absolute effect size threshold of ∣risk_nes∣≥0.5. To anchor our 
CAD interpretations, we specifically tracked peak associations within pathologically relevant 
tissues, including liver, whole blood, and three arterial beds (Figure 3E). Concurrently, GWAS 
Catalog associations were programmatically retrieved by rsID to determine if the prioritized 
variants retained independent, pleiotropic support from CAD- or lipid-related phenotypes (Figure 
3F). 
Crucially, this joint prioritization strategy captured a robust set of regulatory candidates that would 
be difficult to recover using any single model in isolation. Pairwise correlations among the three 
model-specific scores approached zero, yet each maintained a moderate association with the 
joint score, demonstrating that the final ranking integrates orthogonal biological signals rather

Discussion 
s2f-agent addresses a practical and increasingly important problem in computational genomics: 
how to translate open-ended workflow requests into safe, structured, and executable plans 
across heterogeneous S2F model families. In the broader framing adopted here, that family 
includes both direct predictive models and gLM-style foundation models. This problem matters 
because S2F systems are no longer niche benchmark tools. They are increasingly used to 
interpret non-coding variation, prioritize candidate causal variants, reason about transcriptional 
and splicing consequences, support embedding- or zero-shot-driven functional inference, and 
enable downstream translational workflows in clinical genetics and therapeutic discovery. The 
system does not try to replace AlphaGenome, Borzoi, DNABERT-2, Evo 2, GPN, NTv3, or 
SegmentNT. Instead, it provides the orchestration layer that sits above them and standardizes 
how requests are routed, validated, and operationalized. 
This framing is important for two reasons. First, many real-world bottlenecks in applying S2F 
models are not caused by a lack of predictive architectures, but by difficulty choosing among 
direct predictors versus embedding-oriented gLM workflows, supplying the right inputs, 
respecting model-specific constraints, and turning user intent into reproducible execution steps. 
Second, the scientific value of an agent in this setting depends less on rhetorical fluency than on 
whether it makes hidden assumptions visible and whether its outputs can be validated. s2f-agent 
was designed with these priorities in mind through explicit contracts, clarification logic, packaged 
skills, and regression-oriented evaluation. 
At the same time, the current system has clear limits. The evaluation layer is curated and 
case-based rather than population-scale. Skill maturity is uneven, with some workflows stabilized 
and others still in development. The strongest current evidence lies in representative case 
studies and normalized plan generation, not in head-to-head scientific benchmarking against 
alternative agent frameworks. In addition, the quality of downstream execution remains 
dependent on the correctness, availability, and maintenance status of the underlying model 
ecosystems and external APIs. 
These limits also suggest natural next steps. Future work could add stronger self-critique loops, 
richer execution verifiers, and optional external knowledge augmentation for track or tissue 
context. The system could also be extended toward broader benchmarking, larger real-world task 
corpora, and tighter integration between generated plans and downstream artifact inspection. 
However, those directions should be understood as extensions to the current architecture rather 
than results already demonstrated here.
